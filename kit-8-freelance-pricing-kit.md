## WHY FREELANCERS STAY BROKE

Most freelancers lose money in 3 ways:
1. They price by hours instead of value

3. They don't know when (or how) to raise rates

## PART 1: THE RATE CALCULATOR FRAMEWORK

### The VALUE-BASED PRICING FORMULA

**Your Rate = Client ROI × 0.10 to 0.20**

If your work saves/generates $10,000 for the client → charge $1,000–$2,000.

**Steps to calculate your rate:**
1. What problem does the client have?
2. What is that problem costing them monthly? (lost revenue, wasted time, etc.)
3. What is your solution worth over 12 months?
4. Price = 10-20% of that annual value

### PROJECT PRICING (Better Than Hourly)

**Formula:** (Estimated hours × your hourly) × 1.5 buffer

- Always add 50% buffer for revisions and scope creep
- Quote a flat project fee, not hourly (clients hate hourly)
- Break into 3 payments: 30% upfront / 40% mid / 30% on delivery

## PART 3: WHEN & HOW TO RAISE YOUR RATES

### The 3 Signs You Must Raise Rates Now
1. You're booked 4+ weeks out
2. Clients accept your quote without negotiating
3. You're making less per hour than a junior employee

### The Rate Raise Formula
- Raise 20-30% per year minimum
- Tell existing clients 30 days in advance
- New clients get new rate immediately

### Script: How to Tell a Client Your Rate Is Going Up
```
"[Name], I wanted to give you advance notice: my rate 
increases from $[X] to $[Y] on [date]. 

Projects started before [date] are locked at the current rate.

After [date], new work is at $[Y].

Want to get anything started before then?"
```

```

and explain how to present it to the client.

```

that is confident, non-needy, and adds value in each email.

## PART 5: FREELANCE PRICING MISTAKES TO AVOID

1. **Pricing by hours** — Clients pay for outcomes, not time
2. **No discovery call** — Always qualify before proposing
3. **No contract** — Every project needs one, no exceptions
4. **Negotiating against yourself** — Send quote, then be quiet
5. **No revision limit** — Always specify number of revision rounds
6. **Working before payment** — 30-50% deposit always
7. **Underpricing to "build portfolio"** — It attracts bad clients
8. **Not raising rates annually** — Inflation eats your profit
