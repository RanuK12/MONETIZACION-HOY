PART 1: THE ANSWER FRAMEWORK — STAR-P METHOD

Every great interview answer follows this structure:

S — Situation (Set the scene, 1-2 sentences) T — Task (What was your responsibility) A — Action (What YOU specifically did — use "I", not "we") R — Result (Quantified outcome: %, $, time saved, rank) P — Pivot (Connect to THIS job: "This is why I want to apply that here...")

Example:

Question: "Tell me about a time you managed a difficult project."

S: "At [Company], we were launching a new product with 3 weeks left and 2 team members out sick." T: "As project lead, I had to deliver on time without the original team." A: "I reprioritized the roadmap to cut non-essential features, brought in 1 contractor, and ran daily 15-min standups to catch blockers early." R: "We launched on time. The MVP had 80% of planned features and hit 94% of our target KPIs in month 1." P: "That experience taught me that constraints force creativity. It's actually how I approach product decisions — which is why this role at [Company] excites me."

PART 2: 50 QUESTION TEMPLATES WITH ANSWER FRAMEWORKS

BEHAVIORAL QUESTIONS (STAR-P Method)

  1. "Tell me about yourself." Framework: Present role → key achievement → past that led here → why this role.
"I'm currently [role] at [company] where I [key responsibility]. 
Most recently, I [specific achievement with number]. 
Before that, I [relevant background]. 
I'm here because [specific reason this role/company appeals to you]."
  1. "Why do you want to work here?" Framework: Specific company insight → how your skills match → your goal.
"Three things specifically drew me: [thing 1 — research-based], 
[thing 2 — mission/product alignment], and [thing 3 — role fit]. 
This aligns directly with where I want to grow in [area]."
  1. "Tell me about your greatest achievement." Framework: Choose something quantifiable. Use STAR-P.
"The achievement I'm most proud of is [result with number]. 
Here's how it happened: [STAR-P story in 90 seconds]."
  1. "Tell me about a failure." Framework: Real failure → what you learned → how you changed → result after.
"I once [specific failure]. It happened because [honest root cause]. 
What I learned: [lesson]. Since then, I've [specific behavior change] — 
and [positive outcome that resulted]."
  1. "Describe a time you handled conflict." Framework: Describe conflict neutrally → your approach → resolution → relationship outcome.
"I had a disagreement with [role, not name] about [topic]. 
Rather than escalating, I [specific action]. 
We resolved it by [method], and [positive outcome]. 
The relationship actually [improved/continued well] after."
  1. "How do you handle pressure/tight deadlines?"
  2. "Give an example of leading without authority."
  3. "Tell me about a time you influenced someone to change their mind."
  4. "Describe your biggest weakness."
  5. "Tell me about a time you went above and beyond."
  6. "How do you prioritize when everything is urgent?"
  7. "Describe a time you had to learn something quickly."
  8. "Tell me about a time you made a decision with incomplete information."
  9. "How have you handled a difficult team member?"
  10. "Tell me about a time you received critical feedback."

COMPETENCY QUESTIONS

  1. "How do you approach problem-solving?" Framework: Process (steps) → example → result.

  2. "How do you manage multiple projects?" Framework: System/tool → prioritization method → example.

  3. "How do you stay current in your field?" Framework: Specific sources → recent thing you learned → how you applied it.

  4. "Describe your management style." Framework: Principle + example + outcome.

  5. "How do you build relationships with stakeholders?"

ROLE-SPECIFIC QUESTIONS

For technical roles: 21. "Walk me through your approach to debugging a complex issue." 22. "How do you ensure code quality under time pressure?" 23. "Tell me about the most technically challenging project you've worked on."

For leadership/management roles: 26. "How do you develop people on your team?" 27. "Tell me about a time you had to let someone go." 28. "How do you build a high-performing team from scratch?" 29. "How do you handle an underperformer?" 30. "How do you give difficult feedback?"

For sales/marketing roles: 31. "Walk me through a deal/campaign you're most proud of." 32. "How do you handle rejection?" 33. "Tell me about a time you hit a target others thought was impossible." 34. "How do you build a pipeline from zero?" 35. "Describe your process for understanding a customer's needs."

SITUATIONAL QUESTIONS ("What would you do if...")

  1. "You're given a project with an impossible deadline — what do you do?"
  2. "Your manager gives you feedback you disagree with — how do you respond?"
  3. "A key team member leaves mid-project — how do you handle it?"
  4. "You discover a major mistake in a project that's about to launch — what do you do?"
  5. "Two executives give you conflicting priorities — how do you navigate this?"

CLOSING QUESTIONS

  1. "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?" Framework: Growth in this function → contribution to company → realistic ambition.

  2. "Why should we hire you over other candidates?" Framework: 3 specific differentiators, each tied to a job requirement.

  3. "What's your expected salary?" Framework: Research-based range, anchor high, give a range not a number.

"Based on my research and [X years] experience, I'm targeting 
$[X]–$[Y]. I'm open to the full comp conversation — 
is that range aligned with your budget?"

PART 3: SALARY NEGOTIATION SCRIPTS

Script 1: First offer received

"Thank you — I'm really excited about this opportunity. 
Before I respond, can I take [24-48 hours] to review the full offer?"

[After reviewing:]
"I've reviewed the offer. I'm very interested in joining. 
Based on my experience and market data, I was expecting something 
closer to $[X]. Is there flexibility there?"

Script 2: Counter-offer

"I appreciate the offer of $[X]. Given [your unique value 1] and 
[unique value 2], I'd be comfortable at $[X+15-20%]. 
Can we meet there?"

Script 3: They say the salary is fixed

"I understand. Are there other elements of compensation that have 
more flexibility — like [signing bonus / extra PTO / remote work / 
stock / professional development budget]?"

Script 4: You have a competing offer

"I want to be transparent: I have another offer at $[X] that I need 
to respond to by [date]. You're my first choice — is there any way 
to get to $[Y]?"

Script 5: Accepting the offer

"I'm excited to accept. I'd like to confirm: [salary], [start date], 
and [any negotiated terms]. Can you send an updated offer letter?"

PART 5: AI PROMPTS FOR INTERVIEW PREP

Prompt 1: Generate custom STAR stories

My background: [brief description of experience, industry, role].
The job I'm interviewing for: [job title + company type + key responsibilities].
Generate 5 specific behavioral interview questions likely to be asked,
and for each, create a STAR-P answer framework I can customize.

Prompt 2: Practice mock interview

You are a senior [job title] interviewer at a [company type].
I'm interviewing for [role]. Interview me for 10 questions.
After each answer I give, score it 1-10 and give specific feedback:
what worked, what to improve, what detail to add.
Start with: "Tell me about yourself."

Prompt 3: Company-specific question prep

I'm interviewing at [Company name]. Their product is [description]. 
Their recent news: [any news you know about]. 
Generate 10 company-specific questions they're likely to ask, 
and a framework for answering each.

Prompt 4: Weakness answer

My actual weakness is [real weakness].
Help me craft a "weakness" interview answer that:
1. Is honest (not fake)
2. Shows self-awareness
3. Demonstrates I'm actively improving it
4. Doesn't raise a red flag for this specific role: [role description]

Prompt 5: Salary research

I'm interviewing for [job title] at a [company size] company in 
[city/remote]. I have [X] years experience. 
What salary range should I target? 
What are the key factors that justify the high end of that range?
Give me a negotiation script for my specific situation.

Prompt 6: Thank-you email after interview

I just interviewed for [role] at [company]. 
The interviewer's name is [name]. 
During the interview we discussed [2-3 specific topics].
Write a 100-word thank-you email that references those specifics, 
reaffirms my interest, and adds one brief value statement.

Prompt 7: Rejection recovery

I was rejected for [role] at [company].
Write a professional response email that:
1. Thanks them graciously
2. Asks for one specific piece of feedback
3. Leaves the door open for future opportunities
Keep it under 80 words.

PART 6: THE 7-DAY INTERVIEW PREP PLAN

Day 1 — Research - [ ] Read company website, About page, recent news - [ ] Research the interviewer(s) on LinkedIn - [ ] Understand the product/service from a user perspective - [ ] Identify company challenges and recent initiatives

Day 2 — Your Story - [ ] Write your "Tell me about yourself" answer (90 seconds) - [ ] Identify your top 5 career achievements with metrics - [ ] Prepare your "greatest weakness" answer - [ ] Prepare your "why this company" answer

Day 3 — STAR Stories - [ ] Write 5 STAR-P stories covering: leadership, failure, conflict, achievement, learning - [ ] Ensure each story has a specific metric in the Result step - [ ] Practice out loud until natural (not memorized)

Day 4 — Role-Specific Prep - [ ] Re-read the job description word by word - [ ] Map each requirement to a story or example from your background - [ ] Prepare answers for role-specific questions (Part 2) - [ ] Research industry trends relevant to the role

Day 5 — Mock Interview - [ ] Use AI Prompt #2 for a full mock interview - [ ] Record yourself on video — watch it back - [ ] Identify nervous habits (filler words, looking away, rushing) - [ ] Practice until the content flows naturally

Day 6 — Logistics - [ ] Confirm time, format (video/in-person), interviewer name(s) - [ ] Prepare questions to ask (choose 5 from Part 4) - [ ] Research salary range (AI Prompt #5) - [ ] Prepare what to wear, test tech if remote

Day 7 — Final Polish - [ ] Light review of key stories (don't over-prepare) - [ ] Sleep 8 hours - [ ] Morning: exercise, eat well, arrive/log in early - [ ] Send thank-you email within 2 hours of interview
